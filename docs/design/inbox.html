UniSchool · design proposal

The President’s Inbox

One place for everything that is addressed to the president, reachable from any screen. It would replace the two stacks of cards that float over the map, the “Noted” buttons, and a few modals that stop the clock just to deliver news.

The short answer

  • What goes in: anything written to the president, in three tiers. To decide (it needs an answer or has a deadline), Letters (read once, keep for the record) and Bulletins (the toasts, filed so they aren’t lost).
  • How the tiers differ: only To decide puts a number on the button. Unread letters add a dot. Bulletins never raise the badge and clear themselves after a term.
  • What it replaces: the event panel, the note stack (board letters, student demands, milestones, founding notes), the NEXT line’s “a matter waits” pointer, and the research, championship and Deans’ modals.
  • What stays as it is: the summer, the final report, the rankings entry, the 20 seismic decisions and the opening letters still stop the clock. Toasts still pop. The activity log stays the full record.

How it works

Three tiers, told apart by form, not only by colour

The test for whether something belongs in the inbox is whether it is addressed to the president. A request, a letter or a report goes in. Something that just happened on campus goes in the log, and only the headline events among those are filed as bulletins.

Weighing it

What the player gains, and what could go wrong

Gains

  • The map gets both sides back. Two 360px columns of cards no longer cover the campus.
  • Answer from where you are. A faculty offer can be answered from inside Faculty, with the roster on screen.
  • Nothing is lost at speed. Toasts, letters and milestones can all be read later, and defaults are marked.
  • Fewer hard stops. Only moments that deserve it pause the game.
  • One arrival, one urgency signal. A slip, a count and red for the last week, the same for every kind.
  • Room to grow. Rival news, alumni requests and faculty grievances have somewhere to go without adding another corner card.

Risks, and the answer to each

  • Out of sight, out of mind. Cards on the map are hard to miss, and a badge is easy to ignore. The slip, the red last-week count and the optional auto-pause cover this. NEXT can still name a matter in its last week.
  • Less charm. A letter over the campus has presence. The reading view keeps the letter layout and voice. The drawer can open automatically for the first matter of a run.
  • A busier toolbar. One more icon. Alternatively the inbox could take the log icon’s place at the left of the ticker, though that is less visible.
  • More defaults. If answering is optional in practice, more matters will lapse. Showing them in Answered and in the summer review makes that a visible choice.
Building it

A small change to state, most of it in the UI

Almost every item can be derived from state the game already keeps, so the save format barely changes.

New pieces

  • systems/inbox/inbox.ts: inboxItems(s) reads catalogue.pending, events.activeDemand, finance.distress.letters, ladder.unread and the research and athletics reports, and returns typed items with a tier and a deadline.
  • Bulletins come from s.log, which already tags topic and subject. The toasts’ toastsFor filter is reused.
  • Saved state: only inbox.read and inbox.filed (ids). Answered comes from catalogue.letters and answered, which exist already.
  • InboxButton in Toolbar.tsx, InboxDrawer as the innermost rung of App’s Escape ladder, and an arrival slip next to Toasts.

In three steps

  1. Mirror. Ship the drawer alongside today’s cards, with every existing channel still in place. Check that the count and deadlines agree.
  2. Move. Remove the note stack, EventPanel and waitingOnMap. Replace “Noted” with read-on-open.
  3. Demote. Turn research, championship and Deans’ interrupts into inbox items, behind the “Stop the clock for reports” setting.
