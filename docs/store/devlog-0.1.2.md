## UniSchool 0.1.2: an easier first hour, and teaching you can read

Thank you to everyone who has played since 0.1.1, and to the players who
let me watch: a streamer, a playtesting session, and people who wrote in
on the form and on Reddit. Almost everything in this update comes from
watching where you got stuck. Your saves come with you: a 0.1.1 college
opens in 0.1.2.

### A gentler start

The first letter used to hand you three tasks and an order to do them in,
and then the walkthrough did them in a different order. Now it simply
welcomes you to your college. Each step introduces its own thing when it
comes: you choose the first program, each one shown with the professor
who would teach it, and its courses come in once it's founded. For the
first three steps the tabs you don't need yet stay shut, so the screen
isn't competing with the walkthrough.

The skip button now asks before it skips, since it doesn't come back. And
the Students tab waits until your first summer: in week one every need
read low because nothing was built yet, which looked like failure. Now
the walkthrough has you build somewhere to sleep and somewhere to eat,
and the first summer's review shows you how content your students were.

### Teaching you can read

A young college can't hire the country's best professors, so the first
job market is weak on purpose, and your faculty get better over fifty
years. As letters, that read as failure: a search full of C's and D's
looked broken. Teaching, research and course quality are now numbers out
of 100, and a candidate's teaching sits beside your own faculty's median.
A 50 among your 40s is a good hire.

And a professor no longer teaches worse for teaching more. A B
appointment used to slide to C's as their courses filled; now every course
they hold is taught at their own level. The number of courses they can
hold hasn't changed.

### Things that now say why

- **Buildings.** The map used to turn a building red with no reason when
  money was the problem, and sometimes even when you could pay with a loan.
  Now it shows green when a loan or a gift would pay, and when nothing
  will, it says what the building costs against what you have.
- **Satisfaction.** When it's falling, the Students tab names the need
  pulling it down and why: "Housing is the drag: 320 beds, 480 wanted."
  The needs read out of 100.
- **Courses.** A program's next course can be started from its hall, not
  only from the Curriculum.
