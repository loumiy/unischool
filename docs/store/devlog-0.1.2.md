## UniSchool 0.1.2: an easier first hour, teaching you can read, and grounds to garden

Thank you to everyone who has played since 0.1.1, and to the players who
let me watch: a streamer, a playtesting session, and people who wrote in
on the form and on Reddit. Almost everything in this update comes from
watching where you got stuck. Your saves come with you: a 0.1.1 college
opens in 0.1.2.

### A gentler start

The first letter used to hand you three tasks and an order to do them in,
and then the walkthrough did them in a different order. Now it simply
welcomes you: three hundred and fifty students have enrolled, and the
college begins with one building, Founders Hall. Each step introduces its
own thing when it comes. You choose the first program, each shown with the
professor who would teach it ("English, with Dr. Grace Bennett"), and its
courses come in once it's founded, each written by the curriculum
committee before it's taught.

For the first three steps, raising Founders Hall and founding the first
program, the tabs you don't need yet stay shut and the figures are dimmed,
so the screen isn't competing with the walkthrough. The welcome says the
walkthrough takes about five minutes, and the skip button now asks before
it skips, since the walkthrough doesn't come back.

The Students tab, and the satisfaction figure at the foot of the screen,
now wait until your first summer. In week one every need read low because
nothing was built yet, which looked like failure. Now the walkthrough has
you build somewhere to sleep and somewhere to eat, and the first summer's
review introduces satisfaction as the year's average, just as the tab
opens. Health care doesn't count against you until the college passes
1,500 students; its card appears then, and says so.

### Teaching you can read

A young college can't hire the country's best professors, so the first
job market is weak on purpose, and your faculty get better over fifty
years. As letters, that read as failure: a search full of C's and D's
looked broken. Teaching, research and course quality are now numbers out
of 100, shown as where they are and where they're heading ("81 → 88"). A
candidate's teaching sits beside your own faculty's median, picked out
when they would raise it: a 50 among your 40s is a good hire. And when a
course grades below its teacher, hovering the teacher says why: "teaching
63: this course 61 (−2, upper-level)".

A professor also no longer teaches worse for teaching more. A B
appointment used to slide to C's as their courses filled; now every course
they hold is taught at their own level. The number of courses they can
hold hasn't changed, so a full department still needs a hire to grow.
Courses grade a little higher, and prestige rises a little with them.

### Things that now say why

- **Buildings.** The map used to turn a building red with no reason when
  money was the problem, and sometimes even when you could pay with a loan.
  Now it shows green when a loan, gifts or the endowment would pay, and
  says which ("part borrowed"). When nothing will, it says what the
  building costs against what you have, that the board has frozen
  construction, or which professor the building is waiting for. A bench,
  lamp or flower bed set down on a building says so too.
- **Satisfaction.** When it's falling, the Students tab names the need
  pulling it down and why: "Housing is the drag: 320 beds, 480 wanted."
  The needs read out of 100, so a 12 no longer looks like a time of day.
- **Courses.** A program's next course can be started from its hall, not
  only from the Curriculum.

### Grounds to garden

The ground between the buildings is yours to dress, and all of it is free.
Paths come in flagstone, cobbles or brick, each laid in its own pattern,
round corners and along diagonals; hold Shift for a straight run. There
are seven kinds of tree: broadleaf, conifer and ornamental as before, and
now birch, willow, palm, and a cherry that flowers in spring. Lamps and
benches are joined by banner lamps in your college's colors, and by flower
beds, planted in drifts of color, that run together into borders. All of
them go anywhere on open ground, not only beside a path.

The Build menu's Grounds tab shows each one as it looks on the map. Paths
and Trees are one tile each: pick one, and its styles appear beside it.
One eraser takes away anything you've laid, path, tree, lamp, bench or
bed, with a drag on a touch screen too; with any other tool, a right click
takes away what that tool lays. While a tool is out, a line over the map
says what it does.

### The sandbox, and the title

In a sandbox you can now pick up a standing building and move it, and
redraw any building in any of the nine architectures; the Build menu
chooses the style of the next one.

And the title screen has music of its own: a theme composed for UniSchool
by Alejandro Tóth, which plays over the title and the screens opened from
it. Behind the menu there's a new painting of the quad. Thank you,
Alejandro.

### Smaller things

- A long college name on the map's pennant never breaks in the middle of a
  word, at any text size.
