# Trailer research — notes

**Status:** research for the trailer script ([`trailer.md`](trailer.md)),
17 October 2026. Nothing here is published.

**How it was done, and its limit.** The trailers could not be watched.
YouTube, Steam, SteamDB and every press site were unreachable from the
container this was written in (the shell's proxy refused them, and the
fetch tool failed to resolve them). So every note below comes from **search
results' extracts** of articles, store listings, press releases and trailer
editors' write-ups that describe the trailers, never from the videos or the
full pages. Lengths, music and openings were often impossible to find; where
nothing was found, the note says so. Treat every figure as a lead to check
before it goes into a pitch. A follow-up worth doing on a machine with a
browser: watch the first ten seconds of each trailer below and correct
these notes.

## 1 · What makes a good Steam trailer

Eleven rules, from Valve's Steamworks guidance, Derek Lieu (a game-trailer
editor), Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game) and the practitioners they
cite. The sources are at the bottom; all were seen as search extracts.

1. **Gameplay from the first frame; logos last.** Valve recommends that the
   first trailer "features primarily gameplay" and to "save your company
   logos or narrative storylines for further into your trailer once you've
   gotten the interest of a player"; a Discovery Queue viewer "may have
   less than 10 seconds". Zukowski's viewers were most impatient with "slow
   panning cinematic shots, logos, and any footage which didn't have any
   player interaction". Lieu now opens his trailers on a few seconds of
   gameplay.
2. **Genre first, then the hook, then the vibe.** Lieu's order is hook,
   genre, content, with the genre established first; "one of the biggest
   mistakes … is that they fail to establish what the game really is
   before trying to sell you on details like the world, the story, and
   vibe." For a fantasy-first trailer the feeling has to come *through* the
   game's own view: the isometric campus by the fifth second, the hook
   (found it, run it fifty years) by the fifteenth.
3. **60–90 seconds, the best of it early.** Lieu's minimum-viable Steam
   trailer runs 90–180 seconds; most guides land on 60–90. Valve publishes
   no retention data. Zukowski's shoppers often skipped the trailer and
   read four screenshots in about five seconds, so the first ten seconds,
   and any frame a viewer scrubs to, have to sell the game.
4. **Built for scrubbing and hover.** Steam's 2025 player shows preview
   frames on the seek bar and adapts from 360p to 1080p. Steam Labs'
   six-second "micro trailers" are cut from your trailer. Hold a shot 3–5
   seconds; no dead stretch anywhere.
5. **It must work muted.** Valve: "the customer may be watching your
   trailer without any audio." Store trailers are widely reported to
   autoplay muted. There is no subtitle track for store trailers, so text
   is burned in, large, inside the inner 90% of the frame, readable in a
   360p player. "Tell, show, repeat": a card names one idea and the next
   shot proves it. Five to seven cards, six words or fewer, two seconds or
   more each. Watch the cut muted, small, on a phone, before calling it
   done.
6. **A slow sim needs compressed time, a moving camera and visible cause
   and effect.** Time-lapses, seasons, a building going up in seconds, a
   pull-back from one building to the whole campus. Lieu treats a trailer
   like a tutorial: list the verbs, show the unique one first, escalate.
   He starts building-game trailers with one structure and adds them one
   at a time, cutting on the beat, then speeds up once the viewer has it.
7. **Keep the interface in the gameplay shots.** Valve and Lieu both say the
   HUD is how a viewer reads the genre. A wordless, UI-free mood piece is a
   different video (a teaser), not the store's first trailer.
8. **Sell the feeling honestly.** Valve's test: show "what the player will be
   doing in the game and how they will be interacting with the world
   you've built." In-engine footage, tagged *Gameplay*. Cities: Skylines
   II's CGI announcement won a Clio and was still criticised for showing no
   gameplay.
9. **Quotes: exact, permitted, short, and not in the first seconds.** Valve
   bars review scores and awards from base capsules (since September
   2022); no rule was found that restricts quotes in trailers. Steamworks'
   accolades guidance: accurate, used with permission, one or two short
   sentences. Practitioners advise exact wording, never stitched, and a
   separate 30–45-second accolades trailer after launch. Before launch,
   playtester quotes go in sparingly and labelled honestly.
10. **Music first; cut to it; peak; a button.** Lieu: "You should do audio
    first," and "if your music and art are good then you're basically
    fine." A cold open, rising intensity with peaks and valleys rather than
    one flat montage, a climax, then an ending. Steam downmixes trailer
    audio to stereo.
11. **One call to action at the end.** Title, *Wishlist on Steam*, platform,
    release window, held three to five seconds. A studio logo gets two or
    three seconds at most.

**Format.** Steamworks takes up to 1920×1080, 30 or 60 fps, .mp4 (H.264 and
AAC preferred), 5,000 Kbps or more, 16:9. No 4K or HDR support was found.
Only the first two trailers show before the screenshots (since May 2023), and
a Steam Deck page shows the first one. Capture at 4K anyway, so the editor
can crop and push in, and deliver 1080p.

## 2 · Trailers in the genre

Management, tycoon and school sims. What each one shows, from coverage.

**Two Point Campus** (Two Point Studios, SEGA)
- *Reveal (Summer Game Fest, June 2021).* In-engine but not labelled as
  gameplay: students cooking giant pizzas, building robots, casting spells,
  jousting. Real gameplay (the path brush) came days later in another
  show.
- *"Welcome to the Academic Year" (July 2022).* Built on one year of play:
  the summer planning, term-time welfare, and the year-end grade. Push
  Square framed it as addressed to "prospective campus administrators".
- *Launch (August 2022).* An animated intro, then party life ("be the
  college party champion") and comedy. The studio's post summed it up as
  "Create stunning universities / Shape the lives of your students /
  Manage a range of wild & wonderful courses."
- *For UniSchool:* the academic-year trailer is the genre's closest
  template, a trailer organised by the calendar. UniSchool's calendar is
  fifty years. Campus sells courses as jokes; UniSchool should sell people
  and time.

**Let's School** (Pathea, 2023)
- Five trailers exist (announcement, Next Fest demo, PC launch, two
  console). Nothing was found on what they show beyond a caption of two
  pupils in a snowy yard. Sold as an "adorably chaotic management sim",
  though its premise is earnest: a principal returns to restore their old
  school. One review found it "strikingly similar" to Two Point Campus.
- *For UniSchool:* the homecoming premise is an emotional hook that its
  marketing turned into chaos. UniSchool can tell the earnest version.

**Academia: School Simulator** (Squeaky Wheel, Paradox)
- Early Access trailer (2017): designing the school, construction, hiring
  from teachers to caretakers, pupils' needs. "Cheerful yet challenging".
  Nothing found on the 1.0 trailer's content.
- *For UniSchool:* a tour of systems, not a fantasy. UniSchool's faculty
  have faces; Academia's top-down figures don't.

**Football Manager** (Sports Interactive, SEGA)
- *FM24 announce (2023).* Real clubs and players, the tagline "Progress Never
  Stops", ending on a roadmap of feature drops.
- *FM26 reveal (2025).* Live-action match-day scenes under the Premier League
  licence, and early engine footage marked "not final". FM26's redesigned
  interface became its main complaint after launch.
- No narration was found for any FM trailer.
- *For UniSchool:* FM sells the role and the big days through borrowed
  emotion (clubs, players, match day). UniSchool has no licence, so its big
  days are its own: the summer, commencement, game day, the Final Report.
  And when a trailer makes the interface the story, the interface gets
  judged; show one decisive UI moment, not a tour.

**Two Point Hospital**
- The 2018 announcement ("Lightheadedness") was called quirky but showed no
  gameplay; a developer video and a long gameplay video followed. The
  release-date trailer followed one recurring patient, Trevor, through
  several illnesses.
- *For UniSchool:* a recurring subject gives a series of trailers
  continuity. For UniSchool, that is one college, or Founders Hall.

**Others, briefly**
- *Project Hospital* (2018): about a minute; rooms, equipment, staff,
  diagnosis. Sold on sincerity against Two Point's jokes, which is
  UniSchool's position too.
- *Planet Coaster 2* (2024): creative tools in both trailers; by October
  2024 a Steam user complained it "has not been shown off". A preview's
  headline: "a game that made me miss a summer I've never had". Nostalgia
  as the fantasy.
- *Jurassic World Evolution 2 and 3*: Jeff Goldblum narrating as Ian Malcolm,
  "serene and beautiful to terrifying". Borrowed emotion again.
- *The Sims 4* (2013 reveal): sold on emotions, with licensed pop on the
  later trailers.
- *Big Ambitions* (1.0, August 2026), *Parkitect* (about 1:53, 2018),
  *Prison Architect 2*: little found beyond listings. Nothing found for
  *Mad Games Tycoon 2* or *Game Dev Tycoon*.

**What repeats in the genre:** a cinematic announcement with gameplay later
(and complaints when it comes late); humour as the default tone (Two Point
owns it); one character carrying a trailer; borrowed emotion; feature-triplet
copy; a calendar structure.

## 3 · Trailers that sell a fantasy

City builders, cozy builders and legacy games, for how they make a viewer
feel time and ownership.

**Cities: Skylines II** (2023)
- *Announcement:* fully CGI. A seedling's roots become wiring, the wiring
  leads to a lamppost on the edge of a city, and a lamp lights (StrateGGames
  puts it at about 0:21). Seasons pass. It won a Bronze Clio and was
  criticised for showing no gameplay.
- *Gameplay trailer (June 2023):* about two minutes of in-game footage,
  described by its developer as "the city grow[ing] from a humble village
  into a bustling metropolis".
- *For UniSchool:* seed to skyline, but in the real game. A small living
  beginning that grows into a place.

**Civilization VII** (2024)
- *Opening cinematic, "Rediscovering Hope":* a farmer finds a buried sword
  that passes between generations and cultures, and it ends with a rocket
  rising behind the same farmer. Narration lines quoted in coverage: "each
  age has its secrets", "We must learn from the past". Tagline: "Build
  Something You Believe In". Music: Christopher Tin's "Live Gloriously".
- *For UniSchool:* one object carried through time, and a **bookend**: end on
  the opening's place, transformed. For UniSchool, that is Founders Hall:
  alone on a lawn in the first shot, at the heart of a university in the
  last.

**Manor Lords**
- Its July 2020 announcement trailer, by a solo developer, set off two
  million wishlists by January 2024. Coverage praised slick building and the
  camera that comes down to the ground among the villagers. Openings, length
  and music: not found.
- *For UniSchool:* cut the wide view against a low one among the walkers.

**Tiny Glade** (reveal December 2022)
- Wordless building, per PC Gamer: fences, walls and towers drawn over
  pasture; vines climbing; a path dragged into a wall becomes an arch. It
  reused clips that had already done well on social media. Its day–night
  cycle was introduced as "the passage of time". 257,000 wishlists three
  months after the reveal. Early viewers mistook it for a management sim,
  so its store page was rewritten to say what it is not.
- *For UniSchool:* one unbroken building shot carried by the music. And say
  plainly what the game is.

**Frostpunk 2** (2021, 2024)
- *Announcement:* cinematic, opening on a drilling station in the ice with
  the voice-over "The world turned against us, yet we survived."
- *Gameplay trailer (2024):* voices address the player by role, "Steward".
  Derek Lieu called it "elevated to the stratosphere by its voice acting".
- *For UniSchool:* the player has a role and the choices weigh something. In
  UniSchool the board's letters already speak to "the President", quietly.

**Townscaper, Dorfromantik, Islanders**
- Townscaper's launch trailer runs about 47 seconds and calls it a
  "town-building toy". Little was found on the others' content.
- *For UniSchool:* a sub-minute social cut on one phrase of the theme.

**Briefer notes**
- *Crusader Kings III's* reveal was "An Heir is Born": legacy as succession.
- *Unpacking* tells a life through what a person brings to each home.
- *Hogwarts Legacy* followed an owl delivering an acceptance letter: "Your
  letter to Hogwarts has arrived."
- *Farthest Frontier* ("from wilderness to thriving town") and *Anno 117* use
  the same arc.

**Fantasy-selling techniques, and who uses them**

| Technique | Trailers |
|---|---|
| From nothing to plenty | Cities: Skylines II, Farthest Frontier, Lieu's building-game structure |
| One object or place carried through time | Civilization VII, Civ VI's launch, Unpacking |
| Wordless building, the music in charge | Tiny Glade, Townscaper |
| Time compressed through seasons, ages, heirs | Cities: Skylines II, Civilization, Crusader Kings III, Manor Lords |
| The player addressed by role, choices that weigh | Frostpunk 2, Civilization VII |
| The bookend: the opening's place, transformed | Civilization VII |
| Say what the game is (and isn't) | Tiny Glade, Islanders |

## 4 · Where UniSchool can stand apart

1. **Make time the subject.** None of the trailers found sells duration.
   Two Point grades a year; UniSchool grades fifty, then names what the
   school became. The same place in year 1 and year 50 is the trailer.
2. **Be quiet where the genre is loud.** No gags, no gag majors, no
   feature-triplet cards. Longer holds, the campus's own sound, the theme.
   The tone has to read as earnest within seconds, so nobody takes it for
   Two Point.
3. **Its own big days.** The summer, commencement, game day, the charter,
   the Final Report, in place of FM's match day.
4. **One decisive interface moment.** The specialization's four cards, a
   door that closes, rather than a tour of tabs.
5. **The game's own voice.** UniSchool writes like no other management game:
   the board's letters, the professors' quirks, the eras' names, the Final
   Report's sentence. That writing is a differentiator and belongs on
   screen, in short pieces.
6. **End on a sentence, not a score.** "Halifax University: a college known
   first for what its laboratories find" is a legacy. A store page full of
   stars is not.
