## 0.1.4

*The specialization as the board's own page (Plan 110), plain words
across the game (Plan 111), the owner's notes and Stephen's livestream
(Plan 112), and the third review's findings worked through: the old save
that stopped the game, words that hold and doors that land, the young
campus, and the late years (Plans 113–116).*

### The chapters

- **Objectives with help.** The drawer is now a short list. It shows the
  chapter's number and title, a few lines on what it covers, and its
  objectives. Each objective has a (?) button that tells you how. Its
  *Show me* takes you to the place and rings what to click. (112A)
- **Looking around**, a new chapter after *The next course*. Pan, turn,
  tilt and zoom the view once each, and *Somewhere to sleep* opens.
  Going back to the starting view is optional. The map's keys now work
  while the drawer is open. (F7)
- **Courses count.** *A faculty*, *Campus life* and *Running the college*
  each ask you to teach a number of courses: 8, 16 and 24. (112A)
- **Fewer chapters.** *Promises* and *The long game* are gone. *Research*
  no longer asks you to answer the board on the university charter. The
  board's offer comes by itself once a lab is at work. (112A)
- **The chair's letters are folded into the chapters.** With chapters on,
  the chair no longer writes what the objectives already say. With
  chapters Off, the letters still come. (112A)
- **A path in *Somewhere to sleep*.** The chapter asks you to lay a path
  from the residence hall to Founders Hall, and rings the Paths tool.
  (115D)
- **The teams opens with your first varsity team.** It walks you through
  the Athletic Director, every team's staff and its venue, ringing each
  in turn. It says plainly that the board sends Athletic Director
  candidates only after a team goes varsity. In a guided run, the
  Athletics tab opens with the chapter. The tab shows ! while a team is
  short of staff. (112B, 114E)
- **The ticker shows the main chapter.** Open side chapters are listed
  first in the Guide. The Research Park gets a side chapter of its own
  when you specialize in research. (112A, 114E)
- **Show me lands where it says.** The build menu opens on the right
  category with the building ringed, if you can pay or borrow for it.
  *Paying for it* rings the funds button, and *A hall of its own* rings
  the first academic hall. Treasury doors open on the view they name. A
  Show me for a tab that's still shut says when it opens. (112A, 114F)
- ***Seats for everyone* shows both counts** while they differ: places
  taught, and places with courses under way. (114E)

### The summer

- **The summer waits for you.** At week 52 the clock still stops, but
  nothing opens over what you're doing. The summer waits at the top of
  the Inbox and in a note above the clock. Play, Space or any speed opens
  it, so you can finish what you were reading first. (112C, S3)
- **Your first summer is walked through**, one control at a time: the
  price, its lock, the applicants, the line, the class and the offers.
  Each is ringed in turn. Use it or click Next to move on. You can close
  the guide at any point. (112C)
- **The admit line has a handle.** A grip tab with arrows, turned along
  the line, replaces the yellow dot. (112C)
- **The hall is a room**, with walls and a lectern and the seats centred
  in it. The class below it is a table, with each group's share and a
  row for the freshman class. (112C)
- **"In line with your prestige" is yellow**, so the price tags read
  green, yellow, amber and red, whatever your colours. (112C)
- **The tuition slider ends at $60,000.** It went to $100,000, but no
  college's standing supports more than about $40,500. A price an old
  save's board froze above $60,000 is kept.
- **The admit note is shorter.** It no longer warns about crowding the
  beds, and it doesn't repeat the rate when both read the same.
  *Incoming quality* says it is part of your academics score. (113D)
- ***Send the offers* stays in view** on a 1280 × 720 screen. (114G)

### New

- **The board convenes.** The specialization now comes as a short letter.
  Click *Convene the board* to open a full-screen page with four panels,
  one for each pillar, in its own colour. Each panel draws the building
  it gives you, shows where you stand in that pillar and who leads it,
  and says what choosing it adds. Choosing asks twice. A bar at the foot
  of the screen says what you give up. (110)
- **A third way to the specialization.** From Year 20, a college whose
  student satisfaction was 85 or more in over half its years is offered
  one too. With no specialization, the Final Report names such a college
  "a college with a campus life to envy". (116C)
- **The board's question.** From Year 25, the board asks you one question
  a year. Should the college grow? Will you take a gift with a condition?
  Should you found an honors college? Each "yes" lifts one pillar and
  lowers another for five years. If you say no, or don't answer, nothing
  changes. (116D)
- **A drive to Founders Hall**, laid from the road to its front door when
  you place the hall. (115B)
- **The Arena has a front**, with a sign, arched windows and your
  college's colours, and each expansion adds a part you can see. (115F)

### Changed

- **Plain words.** Most of the game's text is rewritten: the walkthrough,
  the chapters, the NEXT line, the letters, the summer, the help, the
  tooltips and the events. It talks to you, keeps to one idea a
  sentence, and keeps flavour apart from instructions. Each promise now
  says what keeping it and missing it does. (111)
- **Buttons that ask first say what happens**, such as "Move English (no
  classes for 4 weeks)". Red is kept for what you can't undo: closing a
  program, demolishing a building, dismissing someone. (111E, S3)
- **Fewer pauses late in a run.** A new matter pauses the clock only if
  it has two weeks or less to answer. A matter you haven't opened still
  pauses it in its last week. The setting is now *Pause for an urgent
  matter*. (116B)
- **The calendar counts the year's weeks**, 1 to 52, as the chapters do.
  (112E)
- **The map opens on your campus**, not the middle of the land. A new
  college opens close on Founders Hall, and the view widens as the campus
  grows. Home goes back to that view. (115A)
- **Walkers stay near Founders Hall** while it stands alone, instead of
  heading for the road. (115C)
- **Every program moves the same way.** A program's tile lists its moves
  in the open, one button for each hall with room. (112F, S3)
- **The last letter grades are gone.** The Faculty plot's lines are
  numbers, and training says each professor moves up one band. A team's
  rank reads "#7 of 100", not "7/100". (114C)
- **Prestige in one set of words**, in the drawer, History and the year
  in review: where it's heading, and what each pillar adds. A pillar at
  zero says what opens it. (114B)
- **The briefings' numbers come from the game**, so they stay true. A few
  lines that were wrong are fixed, such as how long a course takes to
  write and when the law school opens. (114A)
- **The Curriculum opens only the rows you can act on.** The rest start
  folded. (114H)
- **Small things.** The search button says why its price grows. Founding
  says its price is the program's first course. The Paths tile says that
  students wear tracks in the lawn. Enter founds your college on the
  founding screen. A search that finds someone says so in a toast.
  (114G)
- **The founding and title screens fit a 1280 × 720 laptop.** (115G)
- **No rotate handle on a picked-up building.** Press R to turn it. On a
  touch screen, the placing bar keeps its Rotate button. (S3)
- **On the map.** Snow falls on the newer roofs: the field house, the
  natatorium, the Law School, the landmarks and the gazebo (115E). Every
  building that takes an added story draws it, towers included. The
  Grand Quad's flowers match the flower beds, and the Student Center's
  clock clears its entrance. The dots over a hall's courses ring silver
  for established and gold for distinguished, not red (112D).
- **Benches on the quad.** You can set out benches, lamps and flower beds
  on a first quad's lawn. The Grand Quad's planted panels take none.
  (115H)
- **An unlocked architecture's letter shows its buildings**: an academic
  hall, a residence, the Student Center and the library. (112D)

### Fixed

- **A 0.1.2 save on an old walkthrough step stopped the game** on
  Continue in 0.1.3. It now loads and plays on. A save on the old
  appointing step counts the programs on offer and asks you to appoint a
  professor. (113A–B)
- **Athletics came before its chapter.** The mascot letter and the
  varsity petition now wait for their chapters. (113C)
- **A named team lost its name** when you appointed the Athletic
  Director. It keeps it now. (113C)
- **The year in review's class line** now describes the class the
  summer records. (114D)
- **The objectives drawer, the log and the milestones** no longer run
  over the strip at the bottom of the screen.

### Saves

A save from 0.1.3 opens in 0.1.4. A run in *Promises* or *The long game*
moves on past them, and a save made at the summer opens with the summer
waiting for you.

## 0.1.3 — 21 October 2026

*The first five years rebuilt as chapters (Plan 106), the owner's notes
after playing them (Plan 107), the summer's admissions as a page of its
own (Plan 108), the Treasury and the Faculty redrawn, and F5's and S2's
bugs fixed.*

### The first five years

- **Chapters with objectives.** The walkthrough now ends once the first
  program is founded. From there, the first five years run as chapters,
  one or two a season: *The next course*, *Somewhere to sleep*, *Seats
  for everyone*, *Paying for it*, *The first summer*, *Standing*, *A
  faculty*, *A hall of its own*, *Campus life*, *Promises*, *Running the
  college* and *The long game*. Each has a few objectives that tick
  themselves when they're met. (106A, C–F)
- **The NEXT bar opens.** Click the chapter's chip, or press J, and the
  bar opens into a drawer: the chapter's objectives with their counts, a
  short briefing (what it is, how, and why it matters) with *Show me*,
  what else is waiting, and the milestones folded in, replacing the
  ticker's milestone chip. (106B)
- **Tabs open with their chapter.** In a guided run, a tab opens when the
  chapter that explains it begins, and the board's matters, the students'
  demands and the Athletics tab wait for theirs. (106E–F)
- **Side chapters for later systems**: *The teams* from year 4 once a
  sport club forms, and *Research*, *The alumni*, *Capital projects* and
  *A specialization* when each first comes within reach. They wait for
  nothing and nothing waits for them. (106F, H)
- **The Guide.** Every briefing you've met can be read again from the
  drawer, and each tab's header has a Guide button to the chapter that
  introduced it. (106G)
- **Objectives: Guided, Brief or Off**, in Settings. Brief keeps the
  objectives to one line; Off is the ticker as it was, and nothing waits
  for a chapter. (106B)
- **The founding faculty start on the payroll.** Dr. Bennett, Dr. Iyer
  and Dr. Okafor are appointed at the founding, and the funds carry their
  first year. The first program can be any of the three on offer, and the
  first letter names them. (107A)

### New

- **Admissions day.** The summer's admissions leave the inbox for a page
  of their own. You still set the price blind, then the applicants rain
  in as a cloud on a GPA by SAT plot, coloured by cohort. The admit rate
  is a line across the cloud, the money, the mood and the tightest need
  update beside it, and the admitted class pours into the hall's seats.
  The decisions are the same two as before. (108A)
- **Prestige at a glance.** History › Prestige shows the figure and which
  way it's heading, a track to 150, and a meter for each pillar, as long
  as the most it can add and filled as far as you've earned it. The
  detail is under *Details*. (107D)
- **The Treasury as one board.** The year's revenue and expenses down the
  left, each line saying what drives it; cash and the year's net across
  the years in the middle; and the levers on the right (tuition, the
  endowment draw, maintenance funding), with what a year at those rates
  comes to. The endowment and the buildings have views of their own.
- **The faculty as a table or a plot.** A Cards / Table / Plot switch over
  the Faculty and the market. The table reads ninety professors in two
  screens and sorts by any column; the plot puts every professor at their
  teaching and research, with the faculty's medians across it and the
  market's candidates as rings.
- **A lab's news on the map.** A paper, a grant or a breakthrough rises
  over its lab the week it lands.

### Changed

- **Prestige moves early.** A new college opens at 32 and climbs faster
  through its first five years, and some of each year's step comes week
  by week, so the figure moves before the first summer. From year 10 on
  it's paced as before. (107F)
- **Crowding is gone.** Prestige no longer drops for a campus short of
  beds, meals or health care, and the applicant pool no longer shrinks
  for it: satisfaction already counts them, and word of mouth carries it.
  Class seats are now part of the academic need, named when they're what
  pulls satisfaction down. (107E)
- **A young college's applicants improve as it grows.** At a fair price,
  the share of strong applicants climbs from prestige 30 to 75, so the
  incoming class gets better in the early decades instead of holding
  still. (108B)
- **The admit rate stays where you put it.** The slider used to open at
  86% with its thumb at the room's edge; now the figure, the thumb and the
  offer agree, and the note says when the room limits it. (107G)
- **Unlocks happen on the spot.** The Students tab opens on Year 2,
  Week 1, and a team granted varsity finds its venue in the build menu
  straight away. (107G)
- **The Faculty tab is chapter 8's**, and a search is one way to fill a
  post, not the only one. (107B)
- **Curriculum rows stay open** while a program has courses to develop,
  and fold when it's done. The filter is *Below top band*, with the band's
  colour beside it. (107C, H)
- **The NEXT line** names only buildings the build menu has, asks for a
  library only when one would help (with one standing and enough seats,
  it points to the teaching), and never aims a school at Founders Hall.
- **Fewer repeated letters.** In a guided run, *Summer is coming* and *A
  hall of its own* give way to the chapters that say the same.

### Fixed

- **A claimed hall's empty slots refused every program** late in a run,
  when its school had nothing left to found. Such a hall now takes the
  college's offers, and when the whole catalog is founded the "+" says
  so. (F5)
- **The Research Park didn't appear** after choosing the research
  specialization until every lab had finished a project. It now opens
  with the choice, as the other specializations' buildings do. (F5)

### Saves

A save from 0.1.2 opens in 0.1.3. Its chapters are caught up to its date,
and a run still on one of the old walkthrough steps plays on from there.

## 0.1.2 — 16 October 2026

*From the first players' notes since 0.1.1, sequenced in Plan 104, with
the grounds, the sandbox and the title theme that landed beside it.*

### The first hour

- **A gentler start.** The welcome introduces the college and Founders
  Hall, with no list of tasks to remember. Step two is choosing the first
  program, each shown with the professor who would teach it ("English,
  with Dr. Grace Bennett"); courses come in when the program is founded.
  (104L)
- **A quieter screen for the first three steps.** While you raise Founders
  Hall and found the first program, the tabs you don't need yet are shut
  and the figures dimmed. (104F)
- **The walkthrough's skip asks first**: "Skip the walkthrough? It doesn't
  come back this run." The welcome says it takes about five minutes.
  (104C)
- **The Students tab opens after the first summer**, with the satisfaction
  figure. In week one every need read low because nothing was built yet;
  now the walkthrough has you build somewhere to sleep and somewhere to
  eat, and the first summer's review introduces satisfaction as the year's
  average. Health shows once the college is big enough to need care.
  (104M)

### New

- **Develop a program's next course from its hall.** Click a program in a
  hall: its summary has the same Develop button as the Curriculum. (104A)
- **What pulls satisfaction down.** While satisfaction is falling, the
  Students tab and the figure's hover name the need that pulls hardest,
  and why: "Housing is the drag: 320 beds, 480 wanted." (104I)
- **Decorate the grounds**, free. Paths come in flagstone, cobbles or
  brick, each laid in its own pattern, diagonals included. Four trees join
  the broadleaf, conifer and ornamental: birch, willow, palm, and a cherry
  that flowers in spring. Banner lamps in the college's colors and flower
  beds, planted in drifts of color, join lamps and benches, and all of
  them stand anywhere on open land, not only beside a path.
- **The Grounds tab shows each tool as it looks on the map.** Paths and
  Trees are one tile each, with their styles beside them once picked. One
  eraser deletes any of it (path, tree, lamp, bench or bed) by a stroke,
  on a touch screen too, in place of *Erase path* and *Fell trees*; with
  any other tool, a right click deletes what that tool lays. While a tool
  is out, a line over the map says what it does: Shift for a straight run,
  right click to delete, Esc to cancel.
- **Sandbox: move and restyle.** In a sandbox, a standing building can be
  moved (on the same rules as a new one) and drawn in any of the nine
  architectures; the Build menu picks the style of the next one, and the
  curriculum committee is set aside.
- **A title theme** by Alejandro Tóth plays on the title screen and the
  screens opened from it, over a new painting of the quad. The credits
  name him under Music.

### Changed

- **Teaching as numbers, not letters.** A professor's teaching and
  research, and a course's or program's quality, are numbers out of 100
  ("81 → 88": now, and where they are heading). A young college's market
  is weak by design, and as letters a search of C's and D's looked broken;
  a candidate's teaching now sits beside your own faculty's median, warm
  when they would raise it. The Provost's policy is *Staff the weakest
  courses first*, and the Curriculum's filter *Below 78*. (104G)
- **No teaching overload.** A professor teaches every course in their
  slots at their own teaching: a B appointment no longer teaches C's as
  they take more on. The slots stay, so a full department still needs a
  hire to grow. Courses grade a little higher, and prestige rises a little
  with them. (104J)
- **A refused building says why.** A building you'd pay for with a loan,
  gifts or the endowment shows green, and says how it would be paid; one
  you can't put up says what it costs against what you have, or that the
  board has frozen construction. A bench refused on a building says so
  too. (104H)
- **Needs read out of 100** on the Students tab and in the next-step line.
  (104I)
- **A long college name on the pennant** never breaks a word, at any text
  size. (104K)

### Saves

A save from 0.1.1 opens in 0.1.2 as it was.

## 0.1.1 — 13 October 2026

### New

- **Save slots.** Three slots beside the autosave, under *Save slots…* in
  the main menu and on the title screen. Each shows its college, its year
  and when it was saved, and can be saved over, loaded or deleted. (98D)
- **A longer opening.** After Founders Hall, the first professor and the
  first program, the walkthrough now shows you how to develop a program's
  next course, where to read how the students feel, and how to fix it with
  a residence hall and a dining hall. Then the college is yours. (103)
- **Halls belong to a school once they hold three of its programs.** An
  open hall offers the three programs on offer; once three of one school's
  programs are in it, it offers only that school's, and the map names the
  hall for it. An open hall's panel shows how close a school is. (100)
- **Closing a program.** A program that isn't earning its place can be
  closed from its hall: its courses end, its teachers are free for other
  courses, and it can be founded again later from the offers. The alumni
  notice. (101H)
- **Named funds.** Some gifts now endow a fund with a name. A scholarship
  fund pays student aid, which lowers the price families see at the
  summer; a chair pays toward a professor's salary, and their page says
  they hold it. The Treasury lists every fund. (101F)
- **A gazebo**, opening when the college reaches a town's worth; the
  Japanese Garden now opens with a small city's. (99)
- **A notice when you earn a new architecture style,** and a *New* mark on
  it at the start of your next college. (98C)
- **A list of the keyboard shortcuts** in Settings. (98F)

### Events that do what they say (101)

- **The college remembers how it answered.** Settle a matter (replace the
  boiler, adopt term limits) and it stays settled. Put it off, and it comes
  back, remembering the year you put it off.
- **Repairs land where the letter says**: a roof deferred on a hall is a
  repair owed by that hall, priced for its size.
- **Hires are real people.** A vacant post, a long-serving visiting
  professor or a colleague's spouse can be appointed, by name, at the
  market's pay.
- **Retirements name the professor who is retiring**, including the last
  of the founding faculty.
- **Campaigns, gifts and things on the map:**
  - an annual fund drive can launch a real campaign;
  - a donor's statue is paid for and waits in your inbox to be placed;
  - a booster club's gift goes to the athletics department;
  - students' worn shortcut across the lawn can be paved;
  - ivy you leave on a building, and a sinkhole you fence, stay on the
    map until the repairs are paid.
- **Smaller fixes:** lowering the endowment's draw moves the draw rate; a
  library acquisition adds a floor to the library; a bequest for the
  buildings pays the repairs owed and keeps the rest for building.

### Changed

- **Prestige shows where it is and which way it's going**, with an arrow,
  in place of the target it was heading for. (98B)
- **The Final Report's titles** read the standings they name. (98F)
- **The share card** looks the same on every machine. (98F)
- **Ivy** is drawn as climbing vines. (101E)
- **Entrance steps** run the right way from every side of a building. (99)
- **MED550 is Internal Medicine.** (102)
- **Balance:** the job market and the pull on applicants read the research
  and student-life standings the college has built. (98E)

### Saves

A save from 0.1.0 opens in 0.1.1. A save made in 0.1.1 won't open in
0.1.0.

## 0.1.0 — 8 October 2026

The first public build: an early playtest on itch.io. Found a college and
run it for fifty years: its campus, its curriculum and faculty, its
admissions and money, its student life and athletics, its research, and
its place in the guide. At fifty years, a Final Report grades the run.
