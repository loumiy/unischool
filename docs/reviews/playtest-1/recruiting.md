# Playtest 1 — finding players

*Where to post, and what to say (Plan 97I). The goal is **about 100
players** (the owner's, 8 October): enough to see where runs stop, and
few enough to read every reply. A quiet run: no press, no paid ads, no
Steam page yet.*

## Where

Read each community's rules before posting: most allow a developer's own
post only in a set thread, on a set day, or with a flair, and some not at
all. Post as yourself, say it's your game, and answer every comment.

| Where | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **r/tycoon** | Management players, the closest audience. | Check the self-promotion rule; a playtest asking for feedback is the easiest kind to allow. |
| **r/CityBuilders** | Builders who like an isometric map. | Lead with the campus picture. |
| **r/playmygame** | Exists for this. | Small, but the readers came to give feedback. |
| **r/IndieGaming** | Large. | Use its feedback or self-promotion thread, not a post of its own. |
| **itch.io's community boards** | itch.io players, already on the site. | The *Release Announcements* and *Playtesting* boards. |
| **Management-game Discords** | Players of Two Point, Football Manager, Civilization, Prison Architect. | Ask a moderator first; a channel for self-promotion usually exists. |
| **Friends** | Who play those games. | The best feedback, and the first ten players. |

**When:** spread it over two weeks, not one day. Friends first (week one),
so the obvious problems are found by people who'll forgive them; Reddit and
the boards in week two. Space the Reddit posts a few days apart, so
replies can be answered.

## What to say

The page's first line sets the tone: an early playtest, free, and the
player's feedback decides what changes. A short post, a picture, the link,
and one question.

**A Reddit post** (pick the picture to suit the community: the campus for
r/CityBuilders, the specialization cards or the admissions card for
r/tycoon):

> **Title:** I made a university management game, fifty years from a
> small college to a university, and I'd love feedback on the playtest
>
> UniSchool is a management game that plays in the browser. You found a
> college with three programs and run it for fifty years: build the campus,
> hire the faculty, set each summer's tuition before you see who applies,
> and at some point choose the one thing your university will be the very
> best at, for good.
>
> It's an early playtest build and free. The thing I most want to know is
> where you stopped and why. There's a two-minute feedback form in the
> game's menu, or just reply here.
>
> [link to the itch.io page]

**A Discord message:**

> Hi! I'm making UniSchool, a university management game (fifty years, a
> campus to build, a faculty of named people, one permanent
> specialization). It's in a free playtest on itch.io and I'm looking for
> players who like Two Point or Football Manager to tell me where it drags
> or confuses. [link] — the menu has a short feedback form. Thank you!

**To friends:** the same, plus what you'd especially like them to try
(the first hour, the first summer, getting to the specialization).

## What not to say

- Don't put "AI" beside "school simulator" anywhere (the second review's
  §2). If someone asks how it was made, the page's *How UniSchool is made*
  section answers it; link it rather than retyping it.
- Don't promise features, dates or a price. Say what it does today.
