# 6. Marketability

Plan 73, area 6. Commit read: `58fa3fd`. Written as a memo to a small studio's greenlight meeting.

**About the sources.** Every market claim below cites a public source. The research ran on 27 September 2026 from this review's container, whose network policy blocks the Steam store, SteamDB, Steamworks, itch.io, Wikipedia, PC Gamer and GameSpot. So the figures come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from the pages themselves. Treat every number as a lead to check before it goes into a pitch deck. Review counts in particular are often language-filtered snippets.

Where the memo gives a judgment rather than a fact, it is labelled **the reviewer's judgment**. The reviewer is a model; this is a desk study, not market testing.

## The recommendation, first

UniSchool is a deep, text-rich university management game that runs in the browser and plays a fixed 50-year run to a graded ending. It is shippable as a niche premium indie game **once three things change**:
1. It has a picture a stranger wants to click: seasons, the landmarks (area 1), a title screen with a campus on it.
2. A run offers choices that exclude each other (area 4, A4-1).
3. Its AI-assisted writing is disclosed and its human direction is made visible.

**Recommended route:**
- A free web demo (the first ten years) as the discovery engine.
- A Steam page from day one of the demo.
- The February 2027 Next Fest if the playtest (70L) and the fixes above land by early January. Otherwise June 2027.
- A premium launch at **$14.99**.

The reasoning follows.

## 1. The niche: what it is and who plays it

- **The market UniSchool sits in.** Management and simulation is a durable Steam category. Simulation takes 9.76% of Steam revenue among games over $1M gross, fourth of the genres [GameDiscoverCo, via Game World Observer, 1 Jul 2025](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/07/01/analytics-three-quarters-of-game-revenue-on-steam-comes-from-action-and-rpgs). The "crafty-buildy-strategy-simulation" games do well on Steam because systems give hours of play from reusable content [How To Market A Game, 27 Dec 2024](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/12/27/what-are-crafty-buildy-strategy-simulation-games/).
- **The school sub-niche is real but small.** Its hits:
  - Two Point Campus: about 8,000 Steam reviews, 1M players in two weeks including Game Pass.
  - Let's School: about 7,000 reviews, 100,000+ players.
  - Academia: School Simulator: about 3,000 reviews, over $1M gross by Dec 2019, bought by Paradox in 2022.

  Sources in the competitor table.
- **The field is crowded.** Over 19,000 games released on Steam in 2025, almost half with fewer than 10 reviews [SteamDB via KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/joao-silva/steam-data-shows-over-19000-games-released-in-2025/). 608 of 20,282 reached 1,000 reviews [GamesRadar, early 2026](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/).
- **Who plays it (the reviewer's judgment).** Players of Game Dev Tycoon, Academia and Two Point Hospital who want more numbers and less slapstick. Players who read patch notes and like a graded run. Educators and students curious about how universities work. It is not for the Two Point audience that comes for jokes and 3D art.

**What the others have that UniSchool lacks (the reviewer's judgment):**
- hand-made art and animation;
- humour;
- sandbox or endless modes;
- console reach.

**What UniSchool has that none of them does:**
- a real university catalogue (431 courses in 7 schools);
- a yearly admissions cycle with a blind price;
- research, varsity athletics and a league of 100 rivals;
- five architectural styles;
- a fixed run with a verdict at the end;
- zero install.

## 3. What would stop it earning

1. **Discovery.** Almost half of 2025's Steam releases got fewer than 10 reviews (above). A page needs roughly 7,000–10,000 wishlists at launch to have a chance [How To Market A Game, 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/09/26/how-many-wishlists-should-i-have-when-i-launch-my-game/). Launch-week sales run at about 0.10× wishlists for games above $10 [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions). The game has no audience yet.
2. **The first screenshot.** The map is procedural and tidy, but in the reviewer's judgment it doesn't yet sell itself against Two Point's art:
   - a late campus looks derelict (area 1, A1-6);
   - the vernaculars converge (A1-1);
   - the landmarks look unfinished (A1-4);
   - the title screen shows no campus (area 3).

   A store page lives on its first screenshot and trailer.
3. **Replay value.** Four of the seven goal strategies end as the same college (area 4, A4-1). A game with a fixed ending needs a reason to play again. At the moment the reason is a better grade on the same shape.
4. **Session length against the refund window.** Steam refunds any purchase within 14 days and under 2 hours played [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/). Short games have suffered for it: *Paddle Paddle Paddle* saw about 21% refunds on about 270,000 copies despite a 90% rating ([Kotaku, Jul 2026](https://kotaku.com/steam-indie-short-pc-refund-paddle-paddle-paddle-zoroarts-2000712822)).

   A UniSchool run is 50 years × 52 weeks. That is 3.6 hours of clock at 1×, 1.8 at 2× and less at the faster speeds, plus reading. A player who finishes a run in about two hours at speed has met the refund line. **The reviewer's judgment:** make the second run the draw, through distinct strategies (area 4) and a sandbox after the report. A first run that fills an evening is not the problem.
5. **Web-only form.** Web portals favour 11–20 minute sessions [Poki, 2026](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026), and they share ad revenue 50/50 [Poki](https://developers.poki.com/guide/revenue-deal-types). A 2–4 hour text-heavy game fits a portal badly as a product, but well as a demo.
6. **No sandbox, no multiplayer, no mod support.** Those are the genre's long tail, and none is needed for launch.

## 4. Marketing

- **The one-line pitch:** "Found a college in 1 week and run it for 50 years: every course, every hire, every admissions summer, until the trustees grade what you built."
- **The hooks, in the order they would sell (the reviewer's judgment):**
  1. The summer: price the year blind, then watch who applies. It is the game's best moment.
  2. The real catalogue: 431 courses in 7 schools.
  3. The ending: the Final Report's title ("a research powerhouse that never won a game that mattered"), made shareable. A share card already exists (`src/state/reportCard.ts`).
  4. Five architectures: Georgian to Modern, the same college in each.
  5. The rival league: climb from #55 to #1.
- **Channels:**
  - **Steam**, which is where the revenue is.
  - **A free web demo** on the game's own site and itch.io, with the full game on Steam. That is shapez's route (above).
  - **Management YouTubers.** Academia's sales spiked with JackSepticEye and DanTDM (GameDiscoverCo, above). Let's Game It Out has about 6.5M subscribers and plays sims and tycoons [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/letsgameitout). Splattercat (about 975K) and Aavak (about 236K) cover indie strategy and builders [vidIQ / Social Blade extracts](https://videos.feedspot.com/indie_games_youtube_channels/).
  - **Communities:** r/tycoon, r/CityBuilders, r/HigherEducation for the curiosity angle.
- **The store page and trailer.**
  - Open on a campus through the seasons (A1-10), then the summer's reveal, then the Final Report.
  - The page should say "runs in your browser too" and "one college, fifty years".
  - Avoid the plain tabs: the Curriculum's 2,000-word page (area 2) is the game's least photogenic screen.

## 6. Price

- **The market.** The median launch price of the top 50 new Steam games by first-month revenue fell from $19.50 (Feb 2023) to $15.64 (Oct 2025). "Charging >$25 is getting trickier, as players compare value to the $10–$15 indie titles" [GameDiscoverCo, Nov 2025](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/are-steam-game-prices-dropping-and).
- **Comparables:** Two Point Campus $39.99; Academia and Let's School $19.99; Game Dev Tycoon, Mini Metro and ISLANDERS: New Shores $9.99; web-origin games $3–10 (competitor table).
- **Refunds by price:** about 8% under $5, about 11.9% at $30 and over. The lowest rates are under $4.99 and at $15–19.99 [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-refunds-how-many-should-you).
- **Recommendation: premium at $14.99**, with the free web demo as the funnel. Two reasons:
  - **Below $10** the game is "too cheap to advertise" (shapez's words, above).
  - **Above $20** it sits beside Academia and Let's School with less art.

  **The reviewer's judgment:** $14.99 matches the market's median and the depth. Revisit after the playtest's session-length data: if real runs last 5+ hours and players replay, $19.99 holds.
- **Not recommended:**
  - **Free web with ads**, because a 2–4 hour text game earns poorly per session at a 50/50 split.
  - **Pay what you want**, because it undersells the depth and gives no signal on Steam.

## 7. The anti-AI question

**The facts about UniSchool.**
- Its code and writing were built with an AI assistant, and the repository's own history shows it.
- It has no AI-generated images, audio or voice:
  - the campus is drawn by code (area 1);
  - the music and sound are synthesized in code (`src/components/audio/engine.ts`: "everything synthesised");
  - the only image files are icons and a share image.
- The player-facing text (47,015 words, area 2) is the part an AI-content policy covers.

**What the storefronts require.**
- **Steam.**
  - Valve's January 2024 rules asked developers to disclose "pre-generated" AI content, code included [Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4145017/view/3862463747997849618).
  - The January 2026 rewrite took efficiency tools such as code assistants out of scope. It kept AI-generated content that players consume, including narrative and text [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam) · [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use/).
  - So UniSchool's code needs no disclosure, and its in-game writing does. Disclosure appears on the store page.
- **itch.io.** Disclosure for games is optional, with Code and Text & Dialog as categories [itch.io](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging).
- **Awards and events.**
  - The Indie Game Awards bar any game developed with generative AI, and withdrew Clair Obscur's awards over a placeholder texture [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/indie-game-awards-pulls-two-awards-from-clair-obscur-over-generative-ai-use-we-have-a-hard-stance-against-gen-ai-in-videogames/).
  - Wholesome Games stands "firmly against AI-generated art" [Wholesome Games](https://wholesomegames.com/).
  - Hooded Horse's contracts bar AI art, audio and written dialogue or story [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/i-f-king-hate-gen-ai-art-hooded-horse-chief-says-if-were-publishing-the-game-no-f-king-ai-assets/).

**What players say and do.**
- Developers' views have turned: 52% of game developers now say generative AI harms the industry, up from 30% [GDC 2026](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/).
- Players split:
  - Steam users are split roughly in thirds, and only 8.1% would never play a game that uses AI.
  - They are most accepting of AI in programming, and least in graphics, voice and story.
  - Strategy and base-building players are among the most accepting.
  - Source: [GameDiscoverCo, Jul 2026](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-do-steam-fans-really-think-about).
- The core-hobbyist audience is harsher: 85% below neutral, and AI dialogue 83% negative [Quantic Foundry, Dec 2025](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/).
- Disclosure costs attention. Disclosing games got about 53% fewer reviews and scored 84.6% against 88.3% [Game Oracle via PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/).
- The backlashes on record are about art, voice and cutscenes, among them Jurassic World Evolution 3's portraits, ARC Raiders' voices and Vapor World's cutscenes [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/frontier-yanks-generative-ai-portraits-from-jurassic-world-evolution-3-after-fans-revolt). No case was found of a backlash over AI-assisted code alone.
- A game marketed as "100% AI-generated" (Codex Mortis) drew "slop" coverage and Mixed reviews [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/109355/codex-mortis-is-a-100-percent-ai-made-roguelite-bullet-hell-and-its-proving-divisive-as-hell-too/index.html).

**How to navigate it honestly (the reviewer's judgment).**
1. **Disclose, plainly and first.** In Steam's AI section: "The game's text was written with an AI writing assistant under the developer's direction, and edited by the developer. No images, audio or voices are AI-generated: the campus is drawn and the music synthesized by the game's own code." Say the same in the itch.io description, and tick Text & Dialog. Hiding it is the failure case: Clair Obscur's damage came from the discovery, not the use.
2. **Show the human authorship that exists.** The 73 plans in `docs/plans/` are the owner's design decisions, triaged reviews and choices, dated and public in the repository. A devlog built from them ("why the summer has three beats", "why the price is blind") is the strongest answer to "low developer investment". That is the reading players give AI games, according to a study of 508,192 Steam reviews [Bazzaz & Cooper, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11539).
3. **Spend human money where players look.** Commission the capsule art, the logo and the trailer's music from human artists, and credit them. These are exactly the places the backlashes happened.
4. **Rewrite the text players read most** by hand, or with a human editor: the opening letters, the summer, the Final Report. The audit already shows where the words are wrong (area 2). A human edit fixes both problems.
5. **Don't claim what isn't so.**
   - Don't claim "made without AI".
   - Don't market the AI ("built by AI in N weeks"), which invites the Codex Mortis framing.
   - Don't enter the Indie Game Awards.
6. **Aim at the audiences that mind least.** Strategy and base-building players are among the most accepting (GameDiscoverCo, above). The cosy and wholesome channels are the least, so skip Wholesome Direct.
